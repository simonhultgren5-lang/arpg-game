# The Second Cup
## 1. Scene title
**Depths of Darkness · Act I opening cinematic**  
32 seconds · six shots · real-time Godot 4 · English voice and captions. The final on-screen title is **Act I — The Tolling of Hollowmere**. The Second Cup is the sequence name used by editorial.

## 2. Story purpose
A smith keeps two cups on the sill. Three nights after his burial, her brother stands on the other side of the window. He leaves before she can bring herself to open it. By the time the player walks into Hollowmere, the dead coming back has cost somebody a night they will spend the rest of their life trying to get back.

This is Edda Varge’s recollection, drawn from the existing Edda lore conversation. We catch up with the stranger on the playable road into town after the cut. Maud still owns the question of the bell, the first quest offer and the key. Hask, the clapper and the founding secret remain discoveries for play.

## 3. Emotional goal
At first, the player notices the blue cup because a highlight catches its rim. At the window, the player understands who might drink from it. On the last wide, the cup is still full. Aim for the impulse to reach through the frame and lift the latch a few seconds sooner. Ask a first-time viewer what Edda wanted. “She wanted him to come in” is a stronger read than “she was frightened of a monster.”

## 4. Environment description
Edda’s workroom looks across a narrow lane. Two hand-glazed cups sit on a timber shelf inside a low casement. Edda stands to their left in her work apron. A candle reaches her cheek and the brass-brown surface of the nearer cup. Beyond the glass, the lane is cool and the wood braces of the opposite house disappear into mist. The forge is cold. When dawn arrives, it reaches the outside of the window first.

The bench, cups, sash and pivot are the four pieces that must survive every set revision. Look through their silhouettes at final camera distances. A busy forge full of hanging tools would obscure the returning hand and the empty place outside.

## 6. Camera shots

Coordinates are scene-local metres in Godot, Y up. Negative Z leads from workroom to lane. Lens figures are vertical fields of view. All moves ease to rest. Editorial frames below use a 24 fps reference, frame 0 inclusive and each listed out frame exclusive. The runtime evaluates elapsed seconds.

### 01 · Two places · 00.00–05.00 · 120 frames
- **Camera.** Start (.91, 1.23, −.34), finish (.68, 1.19, −.48), 33°. A slow diagonal slide separates the two handles. Focus the composition on the rims, with the dark shelf filling the lower third.
- **Lighting.** Warm key from the candle at frame left, thin blue edge from the lane on the farther cup. Hold surface detail inside the glaze.
- **Sound and music.** At 2.15 s a small off-camera cloth contact breaks the wind bed. Start the Hollowmere strings quietly. Leave room to hear the timber.
- **Dialogue.** Silence.
- **VFX and cut.** Lift out of black over the first second. Minute flame movement only. Cut on the audience recognizing that there are two places.

### 02 · The witness · 05.00–10.50 · 132 frames
- **Camera.** From (1.77, 1.53, 1.05) to (1.47, 1.57, .76), 39°. Push in behind Edda’s shoulder and lift the sightline from apron height to her ear. The cups keep the geography of the previous shot.
- **Lighting.** A narrow warm edge picks up her braid. The sash divides her shelter from the cooler lane. Keep the exterior vacant here.
- **Sound and music.** Wind recedes under the first word. Lower the music over the course of her first two sentences. Resist the temptation to illustrate burial with sound.
- **Dialogue.** At 6.00 s Edda begins, “Same as every town. People died and we buried them. Difference is, ours don’t stay put.” The second sentence group carries over the cut.
- **VFX and cut.** A slight change in her eyeline motivates the reveal. Hold the window light steady.

### 03 · The visitor · 10.50–16.00 · 132 frames
- **Camera.** From (.02, 1.58, .31) to (.09, 1.60, −.14), 33°. Look past a trace of Edda’s shoulder at the cap in the right-hand pane. Let the last part of the move nearly stall.
- **Lighting.** Blue-grey backlight defines cap and shoulders against the opposite wall. Keep his eyes in the shade of the brim.
- **Sound and music.** The voice and filtered wind carry the frame. His entrance has no musical sting, footstep or knock.
- **Dialogue.** Finish “ours don’t stay put.” Continue, “My brother came back three nights after we buried him. Stood outside my window till dawn.”
- **VFX and cut.** Reveal him by cutting to a new view. He is already there. The intervening glass and atmospheric depth give the audience somewhere to search.

### 04 · The answer she waits for · 16.00–21.00 · 120 frames
- **Camera.** From (−.74, 1.65, −2.10) to (−.70, 1.66, −2.02), 32°. Reverse through the left opening, close to the visitor’s eye height. Stay on Edda after speech ends.
- **Lighting.** Candle-side cheek is warm. Let the other side fall into the room. Her eyes must read through the left opening at both ends of the move.
- **Sound and music.** Finish the line with almost no score. Hold over two seconds of room air after the final phrase. The expected knock never comes.
- **Dialogue.** Finish “till dawn.” Then, “Didn’t knock. Just stood.”
- **VFX and cut.** The blink lands after the words. First daylight begins outside at 18.8 s. Cut away before showing what the brother does next.

### 05 · The latch · 21.00–27.00 · 144 frames
- **Camera.** From (.77, 1.06, −.89) to (.68, 1.09, −.94), 33°. Frame the hand against the vertical mullion. Follow the iron up with a small change of aim. Keep the now-empty lane behind it.
- **Lighting.** Warm light on the pivot and knuckles. Daylight strengthens beyond the casement. Maintain separation between black iron and wet wood.
- **Sound and music.** One mechanical contact at 23.85 s. Open the wind bed as the sash yields. The first returning music is allowed only after the latch has cleared.
- **Dialogue.** Silence.
- **VFX and cut.** The left hand reaches, the latch turns, then the casement opens a few degrees. Morning has replaced the visitor during the cut. The camera never watches him dissolve.

### 06 · The second cup · 27.00–32.00 · 120 frames
- **Camera.** From (1.81, 2.23, 1.70) to (2.40, 2.82, 2.64), 42°. Withdraw and rise until Edda, both cups and the window share one frame. The rising angle prepares the return to isometric play.
- **Lighting.** Dawn carries the exterior. A trace of candle warmth remains at the bench. Keep the title readable over the interior wall.
- **Sound and music.** Allow the town motif to return, without a victory cadence. The gameplay town bed and score enter under the normal area transition.
- **Dialogue.** Silence. Title begins at 27.6 s.
- **VFX and cut.** Hold the open sash and both cups. Fade to black from 31.00 to 32.00, then give the selected hero control on the Hollowmere road.

## 8. Facial expressions
**Edda.** Her mouth rests throughout the memory. She recognizes him before the cut to his face. Direct the first glance toward the height his eyes used to be, then let her focus settle. On “Just stood,” hold the gaze a little too long. The playable rig has fixed brows, a head turn and a timed blink. The hero facial pass should add one lower-lid tightening and a breath released through the nose. Preserve the post-line hold.

**Brother.** His cap shades his eyes. Cast him with a familiar silhouette, not an overtly supernatural expression. In a high-resolution double, keep the jaw slack enough to suggest he might say something if given more time. Never animate a reply.

## 9. Sound design
Perspective stays with Edda, even for the exterior reverse. The room has three audible dimensions: a close hand or cloth sound, her dry centered testimony, and wind beyond the sash. The current cut uses the existing inventory Foley at 2.15 s and the equipment cue at 23.85 s. For final Foley, record the sleeve of a waxed work apron brushing a board and a single iron pivot lifting from a damp timber catch. Capture contact and tail separately. Keep the late metallic contact lighter than a gameplay loot cue.

Route voice to **Voice**, weather to **Ambience**, the two gestures to **Effects** and music to **Music**. The letterbox carries timed English captions for the entire spoken passage. A muted player must still understand the brother’s relationship, how long he stayed, and the significance of the hand movement.

## 10. Music recommendations
The playable cut uses Hollowmere’s existing town cue. Its source player starts at −23 dB, falls to −55 dB under the testimony and recovers toward −24 dB after the decision. These are fader offsets. For a commissioned recording, use a close bowed viol and one open-string harmonic tied to the town motif. Record the bow lifting as a separate tail. Leave 10.5–21 s available to the actor and the room. Audition the last chord against the actual town entry so the handoff feels like walking into the same place several hours later.

## 11. VFX recommendations
The runtime uses a low-density atmospheric fog, Forward+ volumetrics on High, a translucent casement, asymmetric candle flicker and a gradual change in exterior light. For final VFX, confine a slow ribbon of mist to the far end of the lane. Check the silhouette in grayscale. Add sparse condensation to the lower pane and one clean patch at Edda’s eyeline. Protect the hand silhouette from bright fog and glass highlights. The supernatural event requires no particle burst.

## 12. Godot 4 implementation notes
- The scene is directly playable in the editor. The title menu automatically inserts it after loading an eligible starting hero. Completion or skip sets a per-hero flag, saves, then loads the normal world. Esc and the visible skip button work throughout. The normal town scripts own input and quest state after handoff. - The director owns one monotonic clock for six cuts, subtitle intervals, lighting, the blink, latch contact and title. The 3D set owns the performer nodes, two-link reach, practicals and window geometry. Running on an independent stage prevents live combat, hero barks, HUD and pathfinding from entering the frame. - Forward+ enables volumetric fog when the graphics preset allows it. Conventional depth fog also runs on the set. Existing brightness and shadow preferences apply. The wind, score and one-shots belong to the opening scene and end on scene exit.
- The regression runs the real title entry, samples every shot, verifies the reveal and subtitles, checks the raised latch, exercises completion, reload and skip, and checks control returns before quest acceptance. ## 16. Voice acting script
**Role:** Edda Varge. **Situation:** speaking later to a stranger who has asked what happened to town. **Listener distance:** someone standing at her workbench. **Recorded asset:** the existing English `edda_lore.wav`, 12.672 seconds, launched at 6.00 s. The current cut preserves the original take. These directions also serve a pickup or localization session.

| Cue window | Spoken copy | Performance direction |
|---|---|---|
| 6.00–9.20 | “Same as every town. People died and we buried them.” | A practiced answer. Give “we” its full weight. She helped. |
| 9.20–11.75 | “Difference is, ours don’t stay put.” | Dry and matter-of-fact. An ordinary town phrase that has stopped being funny. |
| 11.75–14.75 | “My brother came back three nights after we buried him.” | The change is in “my.” She has just chosen to tell this stranger something personal. |
| 14.75–16.92 | “Stood outside my window till dawn.” | Remember the length of the vigil without stretching the word “dawn.” |
| 16.92–18.67 | “Didn’t knock. Just stood.” | A practical detail she still cannot account for. Land “stood” gently. Leave the silence intact. |

For a pickup, record one uninterrupted take plus separate sentences with two seconds of room tone on each side. Deliver clean mono 48 kHz/24-bit masters. Re-time localized captions and adjust the reaction hold to the actual performance. The brother has no breath, growl or dialogue cue.
